# Demo video script (2:00–2:30)

Upload the video natively to LinkedIn, not as a YouTube link: native video
autoplays in the feed. Put the links in the post text underneath.

Load the app before you hit record, so the first slider drag doesn't lag.

All numbers below match `data/results/` (version 2). Don't reuse figures
from the first version (38 vs 119 reps, 70.9%, 2.4 months); they were
withdrawn.

---

**0:00–0:20 — the hook**

Slow down on the two numbers.

> This sales team grew three and a half times in six years. Revenue grew
> two point one times. Revenue growth never once looked bad. The margin
> fell from twelve percent to one. A simple ratio would have flagged it
> two years before the margin did, and I tested that on two hundred
> histories, not just this one.

Screen: KPI row, then Module A's chart with the warning line and the
breach line.

**0:20–0:45 — how it fits together, fast**

> Five steps, built from a real Ivey case. Diagnose the cost problem.
> Pick the cheapest structure, with travel and lost expertise priced in
> rupees. Optimise who owns which account. Decide how to sell the new
> product. Then price the change and its payback.

Screen: the "How the pieces connect" line, scroll past Module B.

**0:45–1:20 — the slider (one unbroken take)**

> This slider is the guess that matters most: how much margin we lose
> when an account with engineers in the buying decision gets a rep who
> doesn't know the product. Even if I push it all the way up, geographic
> still wins, unless travel gets twice as cheap. And pure product
> specialists never win at any setting, because they spend their week
> travelling.

Screen: drag the expertise-loss slider from 0.10 to 1.0 at travel 1.0×,
then drop travel to 0.5× and watch the Hybrid edge ahead. Watch the Module B bars and the "At these settings" line change.
Let it sit for a beat.

**1:20–1:50 — the optimiser and the money**

> The optimiser says forty-five reps, not fifty-six. But Haryana, the
> most complex region, actually needs two more. One rep there is carrying
> more than double a full workload today. The saving is twelve million
> rupees a year, the change costs four point six, and it pays back in
> month six. For the new product, the answer isn't the sales force or a
> dedicated team. It's dealers: the buyers are architects and builders,
> not plant engineers, and the market is too small for a dedicated team.
> The bigger lever is price: we're about eighty thousand rupees below the
> market median.

Screen: Module C table and the region bar chart, then the payback chart.

**1:50–2:10 — what's honest about it**

> Every assumption is listed with a range, the results that went against
> my own hypothesis are kept in, and the data is synthetic and says so.

**2:10–2:30 — call to action**

> Code and the live dashboard are linked below.

Hold the GitHub and demo links on screen for the full stretch.

Runtime lands around 2:15. If you need to cut, shorten 0:20–0:45.
